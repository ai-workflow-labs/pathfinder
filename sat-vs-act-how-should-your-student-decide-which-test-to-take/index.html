SAT vs. ACT: How Should Your Student Decide Which Test to Take?

September 17, 2026 · By Susan Cornish

If your student has decided to take a standardized test – or is simply keeping that option open – the next question almost every family asks is: “Should we do the SAT or the ACT?”

The short answer is that there is no universally better test. The better question is: which test is a better fit for this student? And the only reliable way to answer that question is to let the data guide the decision.

At Pathfinders College & Career Advisors, we work with students to build a college strategy that fits their goals, their strengths, and their financial picture. Choosing between the SAT and ACT is just one part of that strategy – but it’s worth doing thoughtfully.

Why the “Which Test Is Better?” Question Misses the Point

Asking which test is better in the abstract is like asking whether a student should take AP Calculus or AP Statistics without knowing whether they’re headed toward engineering or business. The answer depends entirely on the student.

Different students perform differently on these tests. Some students find the ACT’s pacing more manageable. Others prefer the SAT’s adaptive format. Some students thrive on the ACT’s Science section because it rewards logical thinking and data interpretation rather than specific science knowledge. Others find the SAT’s Reading & Writing section more comfortable because they are strong readers.

The only way to know which test will serve a particular student better is to have that student try both under realistic conditions and then compare the results.

The Diagnostic Approach: Try Both Before Committing

We recommend that students take a diagnostic practice test for each exam before investing significant time or money in test preparation. A diagnostic gives a realistic baseline score, helps identify which test format suits the student better, shows which content areas need the most work, and gives families real data to make a strategic decision.

Pathfinders partners with College Tutors, which offers free diagnostic tests for both the SAT and ACT. This is a straightforward way for families to get objective data without a major upfront commitment.

After reviewing the diagnostic results, compare the scores using an SAT-to-ACT conversion chart. If one score converts significantly higher than the other, that test is likely the better starting point for preparation. If the scores are similar, the student can choose based on which test format felt more comfortable or manageable.

Common Myths About Choosing Between the SAT and ACT

Myth 1: Colleges prefer one test over the other

In nearly all cases, colleges that accept standardized test scores accept both the SAT and the ACT equally. There is no systematic preference among colleges or universities in the United States. Students should choose based on their own performance, not on assumptions about what a college wants.

Myth 2: The ACT is easier because it’s shorter

The ACT is slightly shorter, but it moves at a faster pace and covers a broader range of content areas, including a Science section. For students who need more time to process reading passages or work through math problems, the faster pace of the ACT can make it feel more difficult, not easier.

Myth 3: The SAT is harder because it’s adaptive

The SAT’s adaptive format means the test adjusts the difficulty of questions based on how a student is performing. Some students find this helps them because harder questions carry more weight and the test rewards consistency. Others find the pressure of the adaptive format more stressful. Neither is inherently harder – it depends on the student.

Myth 4: If your student isn’t a science person, avoid the ACT

The ACT Science section does not test science knowledge in the way a biology or chemistry class would. It primarily tests a student’s ability to interpret data, read graphs and charts, and evaluate experimental results. Many students who struggle in science classes perform well on the ACT Science section because it is more of a critical reasoning test than a content knowledge test.

Other Factors Worth Considering

Score reporting flexibility

Both the College Board (SAT) and ACT, Inc. offer some flexibility in how scores are reported to colleges. The SAT allows students to use Score Choice, meaning they can select which test dates to send. ACT also allows score choice, letting students decide which composite score to report. Families should verify current policies directly with the testing organizations and with each college on the student’s list, as policies can change.

Scholarship considerations

For some colleges and scholarship programs, a higher composite score – whether SAT or ACT – can affect merit aid eligibility. This is one more reason why it can be worth investing time in test preparation once a student has identified which test is the better fit. A modest score improvement can sometimes translate into meaningful scholarship dollars.

Testing policies continue to change

The college admissions testing landscape has shifted significantly in recent years. Families should always verify the current testing requirements and policies for each school on their student’s list before making final decisions about which test to take and whether to submit scores.

The Bottom Line

There is no single right answer to the SAT vs. ACT question. The right answer is the one that gives this student the best opportunity to demonstrate academic readiness and, where applicable, qualify for merit scholarships.

Take both diagnostics. Compare the results. Then commit to the test that gives your student the strongest starting point.
