Give the Gift of a Clear Career Path

November 25, 2026 · By Susan Cornish

Table of Contents

  1. Give them room to discover their direction
  2. Help them connect college to a purpose
  3. Build confidence through participation
  4. Support the whole family
  5. Bring purpose into the conversation with your financial advisor
  6. Give the gift of a clear, confident future

You have watched their interests change, their talents emerge, and their personalities take shape. Now, as your grandchild approaches college, you may be wondering what you can give them that will truly matter.

You want them to find work they enjoy, build independence, and feel confident making decisions. You want the education you help support to connect with a future they are excited to pursue.

That begins with helping them understand themselves: their strengths, their interests, and the possibilities ahead.

For grandparents, supporting that discovery can be a lasting gift.

Give them room to discover their direction

Teenagers hear plenty of questions about their future. Where are you applying? What will you major in? What do you want to do?

For a student who is still exploring, those questions can be difficult to answer. Even strong students with many interests may struggle to connect what they enjoy in school with work they might find fulfilling.

Career exploration gives those conversations a starting point. Students can examine their strengths, learn about different professions, and investigate what people actually do in those roles.

They do not need to map out their entire lives in high school. They need opportunities to explore, ask better questions, and develop enough understanding to make their next decision with greater confidence.

As a grandparent, you can encourage that discovery with a simple message: “I believe in you, and I want to help you explore a future that feels right for you.”

Help them connect college to a purpose

A college list becomes more useful when a student understands what they want to explore and what they need from an education. That understanding creates better questions:

  • Which programs connect with my interests and possible career paths?
  • What experiences would help me test those interests?
  • How do the curriculum, internships, and other opportunities differ?
  • What will each option cost, and how does it fit our family’s priorities?

Exploring these questions before committing to a college gives families more substance for their decisions. A familiar school name or an appealing campus becomes one part of a broader conversation about fit, opportunity, and cost.

For grandparents contributing toward college, this brings greater intention to the gift. The conversation expands from where a student might enroll to what they hope to learn, experience, and pursue.

Build confidence through participation

Confidence grows when young people have a role in shaping their next steps. Researching a profession, talking with someone who works in that field, or comparing academic programs gives a student something concrete to consider. They begin to understand why an option interests them and what they still need to learn.

These are useful skills beyond the college decision. Asking thoughtful questions, weighing alternatives, and explaining a choice are habits they can carry into adulthood.

Your encouragement can make room for that growth. Show interest in what they are discovering. Ask what surprised them. Let their answers evolve.

Support the whole family

College planning brings together people who care deeply about the same young person. Parents may be thinking about deadlines and costs. Grandparents may be thinking about opportunity and legacy. The student may still be trying to understand their options.

Professional guidance can help organize those conversations around shared information and practical next steps. It gives the family a structure for exploring possibilities while keeping the student actively involved.

Before arranging guidance as a gift, invite your grandchild and their parents into the conversation so the experience feels supportive and collaborative. You might say: “We would love to help you explore careers and college options with someone who can guide you through the process. How would that feel to you?”

Bring purpose into the conversation with your financial advisor

If you already work with a financial advisor, you may have discussed how you want to support your grandchildren’s education. Consider adding another question: “How can we help our grandchild prepare to make thoughtful choices about the education we want to support?”

Your financial advisor can help you consider a gift within your broader financial priorities. A college and career advisor contributes guidance on the student’s interests, educational options, and possible career paths. Together, those conversations connect generosity with a clearer sense of purpose.

Give the gift of a clear, confident future

For a holiday, birthday, or high school milestone, consider giving your grandchild support for the decisions ahead. Connect with our team and choose the package that fits your grandchild’s needs.
